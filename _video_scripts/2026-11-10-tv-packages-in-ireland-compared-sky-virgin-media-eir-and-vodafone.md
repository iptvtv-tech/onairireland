[Section: Sky]
Sky sells TV packages that usually bundle entertainment channels together. You can add sports, movies and broadband on top. The exact channels, the box you get, and how long the contract runs change from time to time. So before signing up, check Sky's own Irish site for the current lineup and minimum term.

[Section: Virgin Media]
Same story with Virgin Media. The channels included, the set-top box on offer, and the contract length are best checked directly on their own site, since these details move around. One thing worth knowing - Virgin Media Play, their free streaming app, is separate from the pay-TV bundle. It's free to use on its own.

[Section: eir and Vodafone]
eir and Vodafone are better known in Ireland for broadband and mobile. Their TV options come bundled with broadband plans, but package contents, pricing and contract terms can shift. So go to eir's and Vodafone's own pages to see what's currently on offer, and whether sports add-ons like Premier Sports are included.

[Section: NOW]
If a 12 or 18-month contract doesn't suit you, NOW is worth a look. It streams Sky Sports content through its Sports membership, and Premier Sports plus TNT Sports through its Sports Extra membership. There's no contract. You can subscribe for a month, or even just a single day, and cancel whenever you like. There's also a Sports Saver bundle, which combines both memberships on a 12-month minimum term, for a lower combined cost if you want ongoing access to both.

[Section: Sports rights]
If live sport is the main reason you want a package, it helps to know where the rights sit. Premier Sports carries Scottish Premiership football, the Scottish Cup, LaLiga, URC rugby and the Investec Champions Cup. It's available through NOW's Sports Extra membership, and it's worth checking Premier Sports' own site for other providers. Sky Sports has Formula 1, PDC darts, the men's and women's golf majors, and most Premier League games. TNT Sports has a smaller Premier League package, plus MotoGP.

[Section: TV licence]
Whatever you pick, remember the TV licence is separate. It costs one hundred and sixty euro a year per premises, and it covers any television in the home, including a smart TV used only for streaming. It's not included in any of these TV packages. An Post administers it, so check with them for the current rate.

If eir or Vodafone broadband is already in the house, see what TV add-ons they currently offer. And if sport is the priority but you don't want to be locked in, NOW gives you Sky Sports and Premier Sports with no contract - or the Sports Saver bundle if you want both long-term at a lower combined cost.
