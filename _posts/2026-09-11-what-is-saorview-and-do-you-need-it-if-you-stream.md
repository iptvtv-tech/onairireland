Saorview is Ireland's free-to-air digital TV service, received through an aerial rather than broadband. You don't need it to stream, but it gives you RTÉ, Virgin Media and TG4 channels live with no subscription and no internet connection, which makes it a useful backup in a streaming household.

## Saorview at a glance

- **What it is:** free digital terrestrial (aerial) TV, run day to day by 2RN, the RTÉ transmission network company
- **Coverage:** [2RN says](https://2rn.ie/saorview-digital-tv/) over 98% of Irish homes can get it
- **Cost:** no subscription. You need a Saorview Approved TV or box and an aerial.
- **TV licence:** still needed, as for any TV set
- **Saorsat**, the satellite version for areas with poor aerial reception, closes in early 2028

## What channels are on Saorview?

[Saorview's own channel page](https://saorview.ie/en/get-saorview-tv/) lists:

- **RTÉ:** RTÉ One, RTÉ2, RTÉjr, RTÉ One +1, RTÉ2 +1 and RTÉ News
- **Virgin Media:** Virgin Media One, Two, Three and Four
- **TG4:** TG4 (channel 4), TG4 +1 and Cúla4
- **Others:** Oireachtas TV, Sky News and Challenge, plus radio stations

When channels are added or move, Saorview says you need to do a manual re-scan (retune) on your TV or box so they show up with the right names. Its [help page](https://saorview.ie/en/help/saorview/) has re-scan videos.

## Do you need Saorview if you stream?

Not strictly. RTÉ Player, Virgin Media Play and TG4 Player stream their own channels live, where they have the rights. But Saorview is worth having if:

- your broadband drops out, since Saorview comes through the aerial, not your router
- you watch a lot of live Irish TV, news or sport and want it without an app or sign-in
- you have a second TV that isn't connected to the internet

If you only watch Netflix and other on-demand apps and rarely watch live TV, you may not use it at all.

For the free streaming apps, see [5 free Irish streaming apps](/streaming-services/5-free-irish-streaming-apps-worth-knowing/) and [TG4 Player](/streaming-services/tg4-player-what-you-get-for-free/).

## What you need

- **A Saorview Approved TV or set-top box.** Saorview says the Approved logo is the only guarantee a product will work with the service. Its approved TVs and boxes support HD channels, subtitles and a 7-day TV guide. Saorview's site has a [product checker and retailer list](https://saorview.ie/en/get-saorview-tv/).
- **An aerial.** 2RN recommends a professionally installed UHF roof aerial. An attic or indoor aerial may work in some places; a local installer can advise. Saorview has a coverage checker on its site.

Then run a channel scan from your TV's or box's settings menu.

## The TV licence

Whether or not you use Saorview, [Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/consumer/phone-internet-tv-and-postal-services/tv-licences/) says any TV set needs one, including a smart TV used only for streaming. ## Saorsat closes in early 2028

Saorsat is the free satellite service for areas where Saorview reception is poor. It carries RTÉ, TG4 and Oireachtas TV. [2RN says](https://2rn.ie/saorsat-digital-television/) the satellite it uses reaches the end of its life in 2028 and isn't being replaced, so Saorsat will close in early 2028. 2RN recommends no new Saorsat installations, and says viewers will be told on the Saorsat and Saorview websites and by on-screen messages as the date gets closer. Those channels will stay on Saorview, other TV platforms and online through RTÉ Player, TG4 Player and the Oireachtas TV website.
