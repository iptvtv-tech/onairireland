The Solheim Cup returns in 2026, and Irish golf fans will once again be glued to their screens for three days of tense matchplay between Europe and the United States. With Ireland's own contingent on the European side in recent editions, interest in this event has never been higher here. Here's what we know so far about watching it legally, and how to make sure your setup at home is ready.

## Who Holds the Broadcast Rights

Sky Sports has been the long-standing home of the Solheim Cup for viewers in Ireland and the UK, carrying the event as part of its golf coverage alongside the Ryder Cup. At the time of writing, the broadcaster for the 2026 edition has not been officially confirmed for the Irish market, so treat this as the likely scenario based on recent history rather than a certainty. Once details are announced closer to the tournament, we'll update this piece with the confirmed channel and streaming details.

If Sky Sports does retain the rights, Irish viewers will need a Sky subscription that includes the Sky Sports package, or access via NOW, which offers Sky Sports content on a day, month, or season pass basis without a long-term contract. NOW is a legitimate option for anyone who only wants to dip in for tournament week rather than commit to a full year of Sky.

## Streaming Without a Satellite Dish

You don't need a satellite dish to watch Sky Sports coverage. Streaming through NOW works on smart TVs, laptops, phones, and dedicated streaming devices. If you're setting up a TV for the first time or replacing an old box, a [Fire TV Stick](https://amzn.to/3T7hgI6) is a straightforward way to get the NOW app and other Irish streaming services running on a television that doesn't have smart features built in. Alternatively, a Roku device or Chromecast will also run the relevant apps, so the choice mostly comes down to which interface you prefer and what's compatible with your TV.

For households that still rely on traditional TV reception for RTÉ, Virgin Media, or other free-to-air coverage that might run analysis and highlights, it's worth checking that your reception is solid before tournament week. An [outdoor digital TV aerial](https://amzn.to/3T7hgI6) can make a real difference if you're currently relying on an indoor one and getting patchy signal, particularly for high-definition broadcasts.

## Getting Your Home Setup Ready

Live sport is one of those occasions where a smooth streaming setup really matters, especially with early tee times that might have you watching before work. A few practical checks can save you frustration on the morning of the first session.

If you've got multiple devices competing for one HDMI port on an older TV, a [3-in-1 HDMI switch](https://amzn.to/4h8t3OA) lets you connect a streaming stick, games console, and set-top box without constantly swapping cables behind the television. It's a small purchase that removes a common source of last-minute stress before a big match.

It's also worth double-checking that your streaming device or smart TV has a stable internet connection well before the tournament starts. A wired connection via ethernet, where possible, tends to be more reliable than Wi-Fi for sustained live streaming, particularly in houses with multiple people online at once.

## What to Do If You Hit an Error Message

Occasionally viewers see a message saying content isn't available, and the fix is almost always mundane rather than mysterious. Check that your account's region setting matches your actual location in your NOW or Sky account settings. Make sure you're using the correct app store version for Ireland, since downloading an app registered to the wrong region can cause playback issues. If problems persist, the broadcaster's own customer support team is the right place to go, as they can confirm your subscription status and flag known technical issues affecting the app.

## Keeping Devices Charged and Ready

If you're planning to follow shot-by-shot updates on a tablet or phone alongside the TV coverage, having a reliable charging cable on hand is worth thinking about. An [Anker 240W USB-C to USB-C cable](https://amzn.to/3SSkm2U) is a sturdy option for keeping devices topped up during a long day of coverage without worrying about a flimsy cable giving out mid-tournament.

## Final Thoughts

Once the official broadcast details for the 2026 Solheim Cup are confirmed, Irish fans should have a clear, legal route to follow every session through Sky Sports or NOW. In the meantime, getting your streaming setup, aerial, and cabling sorted now means one less thing to worry about when the opening tee shots are struck.
