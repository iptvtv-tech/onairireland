## Vodafone TV in 2026: Still a Contender?

Vodafone has been quietly pitching its TV service as a simpler, no-fuss alternative to the bigger cable and satellite bundles for a few years now. With Virgin Media and Eir Vision both pushing harder on their own set-top boxes and streaming tie-ins, it's worth asking whether Vodafone TV still earns its place on Irish coffee tables in 2026.

This isn't a sponsored comparison, and we haven't been given review units by any of these providers. What follows is based on publicly available information and general observations about how these services tend to work — so if you're weighing up a switch, treat exact pricing and channel line-ups as things to double-check directly with the provider before signing anything.

### What Vodafone TV Actually Offers

Vodafone TV is generally bundled alongside Vodafone broadband rather than sold as a pure standalone product, and it typically comes with a set-top box that combines standard Saorview-style channels with on-demand apps. The pitch has always been convenience: one bill, one box, and access to popular streaming apps without needing a separate device plugged into the telly.

The exact channel count and app selection has shifted over time, and we'd recommend checking Vodafone's own site or asking in-store for the current line-up rather than relying on last year's package details, since these things do get revised.

### How It Compares to Virgin Media

Virgin Media's TV360 box remains the more feature-rich option for a lot of households, mainly because Virgin has invested heavily in a unified search experience across live TV and streaming apps. Vodafone TV's interface has historically felt a bit more basic by comparison — functional, but not as slick.

Where Vodafone tends to win is simplicity and, in some cases, price, particularly for households that don't need a huge number of linear channels and mostly rely on their own streaming subscriptions anyway.

### How It Compares to Eir Vision

Eir Vision sits somewhere in the middle. It has leaned into flexible add-ons and shorter contract options, which suits people who don't want to commit to a long broadband-and-TV bundle. Vodafone's offering is broadly similar in spirit but the two are worth comparing side by side on current pricing, since promotional offers change frequently and neither provider publishes a fixed "always-on" rate.

### Do You Even Need a Bundled TV Box Anymore?

This is the real question for 2026. A growing number of Irish households are opting out of bundled TV boxes altogether and instead pairing their broadband with a standalone streaming device plugged straight into the television. It's often cheaper long-term and gives you more control over which apps you actually use.

For anyone considering that route, a [Fire TV Stick 4K Max](https://amzn.to/4j6UWJc) is a popular, affordable option that handles most major Irish and UK streaming apps without fuss, while a [Roku Ultra](https://amzn.to/4hbNJFw) is worth a look if you'd prefer a slightly more neutral, no-frills interface without any particular platform pushing its own content at you constantly.

### Where Vodafone TV Still Makes Sense

Vodafone TV isn't obsolete by any means. For households that genuinely want one bill covering broadband and telly, and who don't fancy setting up a separate streaming device, it remains a reasonably tidy option. It also suits people who aren't especially fussed about having the very latest interface and just want something that works.

If you're a heavy streamer already juggling four or five subscription apps, though, a bundled box starts to feel like an extra layer you don't need — especially when a Fire TV Stick or Roku device can plug straight into any telly and give you access to the same apps directly.

### The Verdict

Vodafone TV in 2026 is neither a standout winner nor a service to avoid outright. It's a solid, mid-tier option for convenience-focused households, but it's no longer the automatic default it might once have been, now that standalone streaming devices have become so cheap and easy to set up. Before committing to any bundle, it's worth comparing current Vodafone, Virgin Media, and Eir Vision pricing directly with each provider, since offers and contract terms change often enough that anything printed here could be out of date by the time you read it.
