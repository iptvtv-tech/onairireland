## Vodafone TV: Is It Still Worth Using in 2026?

Vodafone has been quietly pitching its TV bundle as the "add it to your broadband" option for Irish households for a good few years now. With Virgin Media and Eir Vision both refreshing their own offerings recently, it's worth asking whether Vodafone TV still earns its place on the couch in 2026, or whether it's become the option people only choose because it's convenient at sign-up.

### What Vodafone TV Actually Offers

Vodafone TV is built around a set-top box bundled in with broadband and mobile plans, giving access to standard Irish channels (RTÉ, Virgin Media One, TG4 and so on) alongside a selection of on-demand apps. In recent years Vodafone has leaned into aggregating third-party apps like Netflix and Disney+ into a single interface, rather than trying to build out a huge proprietary library of its own.

That's arguably the right call. Building original content or exclusive deals is expensive, and most Irish viewers just want one remote and one bill. The question is whether the box and interface are pleasant to use day to day, and that's where opinions have been mixed. Menu speed and search have historically lagged a step behind what you'd get from a modern streaming stick, though we'd note we haven't tested the very latest firmware update ourselves, so it's worth checking recent user reviews before assuming anything here is unchanged.

### How It Compares to Virgin Media and Eir Vision

Virgin Media's TV360 platform has generally had the edge in interface polish and the size of its on-demand catalogue, partly because Virgin Media has more scale to negotiate content deals. Eir Vision, similarly, has pushed hard on integrating apps and offering flexible contract terms without long lock-ins.

Vodafone's pitch tends to be price and simplicity rather than being the most feature-rich box on the market. If you're already a Vodafone broadband or mobile customer, bundling TV in can work out cheaper than paying for a separate box elsewhere, but if you're starting from scratch and shopping purely on TV features, it's not automatically the strongest option on the shelf. We'd recommend checking current bundle pricing directly with Vodafone before switching, since broadband and TV pricing changes fairly often and older promotional rates you might see mentioned online won't necessarily still apply.

### Do You Even Need a Dedicated TV Box?

This is the bigger question for a lot of households in 2026. If most of your viewing is Netflix, Disney+, Prime Video and RTÉ Player rather than linear channels, a general-purpose streaming device can often do the job just as well, sometimes better, without tying you into a broadband contract at all.

For example, a [Fire TV Stick 4K Max](https://amzn.to/4j6UWJc) plugs into any TV's HDMI port and gives you a single home screen for most Irish streaming apps, along with reasonably fast navigation and voice search. It won't give you live linear channels the way a Vodafone or Virgin box does, but for on-demand-first households it's a much cheaper way to get a modern interface.

If you'd rather avoid Amazon's ecosystem, the [Roku Ultra](https://amzn.to/4hbNJFw) is another solid option, with a simple menu structure and support for the main Irish streaming apps, plus decent remote-finding features if that's ever been an issue in your house. A Chromecast or Fire TV Cube are also worth a look if you specifically want voice control or a hands-free setup, though for most people a straightforward stick covers the basics fine.

### Who Vodafone TV Still Suits

Vodafone TV makes most sense for households that want live TV channels alongside streaming apps, are already on Vodafone broadband, and don't want to juggle multiple boxes or subscriptions separately. It's a reasonable, unremarkable choice rather than a stand-out one.

For anyone whose viewing habits have shifted almost entirely to streaming apps, though, it may be worth comparing the cost of a bundled TV package against simply paying for a standalone streaming device and your subscriptions individually. The saving isn't guaranteed to be large, but for some households it will be.

### The Bottom Line

Vodafone TV in 2026 isn't a bad service, but it's not obviously ahead of Virgin Media or Eir Vision either. It remains a fine choice for existing Vodafone customers who value simplicity over the newest interface, while more streaming-focused households might get better value and a snappier experience from a dedicated device paired with the apps they actually use.
