If you've ever gone looking for a specific Championship match only to find it's not on RTÉ, you're not alone — GAA coverage in Ireland is split across three separate broadcasters, and which one has your match depends on the broadcasters' picks, not the county.

## The three-way split

**RTÉ** shows 35 live Championship games in 2026 on RTÉ2 and RTÉ Player, including the All-Ireland finals, plus The Sunday Game highlights.

**TG4** carries Allianz League games, the Minor and U20 championships and the Ladies football championships live, plus highlights — all free-to-air.

**GAA+** (rebranded from GAAGO after the GAA took full ownership of the platform from RTÉ in early 2025) holds exclusive rights to a further block of Championship matches — 40 exclusive live games in Ireland in 2026 — available only with a season pass or single-game purchase. This is usually where the controversy comes from: it's entirely possible for a big-name fixture to land on GAA+ rather than free-to-air TV, purely based on which games the broadcasters pick.

## What this means practically

- Check the fixture list each week rather than assuming — coverage is assigned match by match
- If a match isn't listed on RTÉ or TG4's schedule, it's very likely on GAA+
- GAA+ sells a season pass (€95 in 2026, up from €79 in 2025) or single games (€15, or 3 for €30) — **check the current price on gaaplus.ie**, as it changes each season

## Watching on a streaming device

Whichever combination you need, the setup is the same principle across services: RTÉ Player and TG4 Player are on most major streaming devices and smart TVs, and GAA+ has its own app for Fire TV, Apple TV, Google TV, Roku, and Samsung and LG smart TVs (2021 models on), plus Chromecast and AirPlay casting from the mobile app.
