If you've ever gone looking for a specific Championship match only to find it's not on RTÉ, you're not alone — GAA coverage in Ireland is split across three separate broadcasters, and which one has your match depends on the round, not the county.

## The three-way split

**RTÉ** holds free-to-air rights to a set number of live Championship games each season under its current multi-year broadcast deal, plus its Sunday and Monday highlights shows.

**TG4** carries the Minor, U20, and camogie championships live, along with its own Monday night highlights coverage — all free-to-air.

**GAA+** (rebranded from GAAGO after the GAA took full ownership of the platform from RTÉ in early 2025) holds exclusive rights to a further block of Championship matches — historically around 40 games a season — available only through a paid subscription. This is usually where the controversy comes from: it's entirely possible for a big-name fixture to land on GAA+ rather than free-to-air TV, purely based on the broadcast schedule for that round.

## What this means practically

- Check the fixture list each week rather than assuming — coverage is assigned round by round
- If a match isn't listed on RTÉ or TG4's schedule, it's very likely on GAA+
- GAA+ pricing is typically a flat season fee rather than per-match — **check the current price directly on GAA+'s own site**, as it's changed in recent seasons and we don't want to quote a figure that's gone stale

## Watching on a streaming device

Whichever combination you need, the setup is the same principle across services: the RTÉ Player and TG4 Player apps are available on all major streaming devices (Fire TV Stick, Apple TV, Chromecast, and most smart TV platforms), and GAA+ runs as its own app on the same devices.
