If you've cut the cord entirely and only stream over the internet, this is one of the most common questions we get — and the answer surprises a lot of people: if there's a TV set in the house, you almost certainly need a licence.

## The short version

Under Ireland's Broadcasting Act 2009, a **TV licence is required for any premises with a television, or equipment capable of receiving a broadcast television signal** — that's the key phrase. Computers, phones, tablets and other devices that can't receive a TV signal by aerial, satellite or cable are **not** covered. A TV set always is — Citizens Information says "You need a TV licence if you have a Smart TV, even if you only use the TV for streaming."

## What actually triggers the requirement

- **You need a licence if:** you have any TV set — including a smart TV used only for apps, a TV with no aerial connected, or even a broken TV — or any other equipment capable of picking up a broadcast TV signal, regardless of whether you actually watch RTÉ
- **You don't need one if:** you have no TV set and watch only on a phone, laptop or tablet, using apps like Netflix, [RTÉ Player](/reviews/rt-player-app-review-what-s-good-and-what-s-missing/) or Prime Video

The licence applies **per address, not per person** — one licence covers a whole house, however many TVs are in it, while each separate flat or apartment needs its own. If you rent and there's a TV, you need a licence whether you or the landlord owns it.

## The current fee

The annual TV licence fee is **€160** (correct at time of writing — always worth double-checking the current figure directly with An Post, who currently administer TV licensing in Ireland, since fees can change).

## Why this trips people up

Streaming apps like RTÉ Player don't ask you to prove you have a licence, and a smart TV *can* receive a broadcast signal if you plug an aerial into it — which is why it counts, whether or not you ever connect one. The practical takeaway: if there's a TV set in the home, smart or not, you need a licence. Only homes with no TV set, watching on phones, laptops and tablets, are exempt.

If you're at all unsure about your specific setup, An Post's TV licence service and [Citizens Information](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/consumer/phone-internet-tv-and-postal-services/tv-licences/) are the authoritative sources to confirm — this guide is a starting point, not a legal ruling on your situation.
