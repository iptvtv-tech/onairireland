**The short answer:** decide which apps your house actually uses, check your TV has a free HDMI port and whether it's 4K, then pick the device that runs those apps. Look at the regular price before Black Friday so you can tell whether a "deal" is real, and keep the receipt (or ask for a gift receipt) in case it needs to go back. For subscriptions, see the [offers we've confirmed](#streaming-subscription-offers) on each provider's Irish page, and check what each one costs once the offer ends.

## Which streaming device suits which household

### A budget stick for an HD TV

If the TV is HD rather than 4K (a bedroom, kitchen or older living room set), you don't need to pay for 4K. A basic stick such as the {% include product-link.html id="fire-tv-stick" text="Amazon Fire TV Stick" %} adds the main streaming apps to any TV with an HDMI port. Check the listing says it runs Fire OS, because Amazon's newest HD stick runs Vega OS, which has fewer apps. Our [streaming devices guide]({% post_url 2026-09-04-best-streaming-devices-ireland %}) compares the cheaper options.

### A 4K stick for a 4K TV

For a 4K TV, the {% include product-link.html id="fire-tv-stick-4k-max-review" text="Fire TV Stick 4K Max" %} is the safe Fire TV pick, because it runs Fire OS, which has the full range of apps. Amazon's newest Fire TV Stick 4K runs its Vega operating system instead, and Vega has fewer apps (more on that below), so read the product listing carefully to see which version you're getting.

### A premium stick or box

The 4K Max is also Amazon's highest-spec Fire TV stick, with Wi-Fi 6E support. See our [Fire TV Stick 4K Max guide]({% post_url 2026-09-17-fire-tv-stick-4k-max-worth-the-upgrade %}) for whether the upgrade is worth it.

### Google TV Streamer for Android and Google homes

The {% include product-link.html id="google-tv-streamer" text="Google TV Streamer" %} is Google's current box. Google's Irish store lists 4K HDR with Dolby Vision, 32 GB of storage and a built-in Ethernet port. It suits homes that already use Android phones and Google accounts. Note that Google says an HDMI 2.1 cable is needed and isn't in the box. More in our [Google TV Streamer guide]({% post_url 2026-09-20-google-tv-streamer-review-should-you-switch-from-fire-tv %}).

### Roku for a simple menu

The {% include product-link.html id="roku-streaming-stick-4k" text="Roku Streaming Stick Plus" %} has a plain, simple menu that suits less confident users. Roku says RTÉ Player is officially available on Roku in Ireland. Check Virgin Media Play and any other app you need in Roku's Channel Store before you buy. Our [Roku guide]({% post_url 2026-09-13-roku-streaming-devices-are-they-worth-buying-in-ireland %}) explains more.

### Apple TV for Apple homes

The Apple TV 4K makes most sense if the house already uses iPhones, iPads and an Apple Account. Apple's Irish store page says it needs a TV with HD capability or better, works over Wi-Fi or wired Ethernet, and that the HDMI cable is sold separately. We don't have an affiliate link for it, so buy it wherever suits you. See our [Apple TV 4K guide]({% post_url 2026-09-12-apple-tv-4k-review-is-it-worth-the-price-for-irish-viewers %}).

Before you sign up to an offer:

- **Check what it rolls onto.** When the offer ends, the membership carries on at the regular price unless you cancel. [NOW's offer page](https://www.nowtv.com/ie/plans/new/entertainment) says this for its own offers. Our [streaming price tracker](/streaming-prices/) has every regular price.
- **Check for a minimum term.** With a minimum term, you pay for those months even if you stop watching.
- **Check what you already pay for.** Some TV and broadband packages include a streaming service. Sky Ultimate TV, for example, includes HBO Max Basic with Ads: see our [HBO Max guide]({% post_url 2026-09-21-is-hbo-max-coming-to-ireland %}).

## The Irish apps on each device

App lists change, so check each app before you buy rather than relying on any guide, this one included. Here's what the app stores and broadcasters themselves say:

- **RTÉ Player:** listed under Apple TV in the Irish App Store, available for Android TV on Google Play (the store Google TV devices use) and listed in Amazon's Appstore for Fire TV.
- **Fire TV models on Vega OS:** Amazon says it's adding apps to Vega, but there are fewer than on Fire OS. Search Amazon's Appstore for each app you use and check it lists your exact model.
- **Roku:** search Roku's Channel Store for RTÉ Player, Virgin Media Play and TG4 Player before you buy.
- **Virgin Media Play:** Virgin Media's own help page lists computers, Android and iOS phones and tablets, Virgin Media and Sky platforms and NOW. For a stick or box, check that device's app store.

Check any paid or sport service you use the same way. Install apps only from the device's own app store. For the full list of free Irish apps, see [free Irish streaming apps worth knowing]({% post_url 2026-09-23-5-free-irish-streaming-apps-worth-knowing %}).

## What to check before you buy

1. **A free HDMI port.** Every stick and box plugs into one. If the ports are taken by a set-top box or games console, you'll need to swap a cable or use a switch.
2. **Whether the TV is 4K.** On an HD TV, a 4K device just shows HD, so don't pay extra for nothing.
3. **An HDMI cable, if the box needs one.** Google and Apple both say their boxes need an HDMI cable that's sold separately. Sticks plug straight in.
4. **Wi-Fi where the TV is.** If the router is at the other end of the house, live sport is where you'll notice buffering. The Google TV Streamer and Apple TV 4K have an Ethernet port; most sticks need an adapter for a wired connection.
5. **Vega OS on new Fire TV models.** As above, check your apps are available for the exact model.
6. **Room behind the TV.** If a stick sits tight against the wall, a short HDMI extender gives it clearance.

## How to tell a real deal from a fake one

- **Know the regular price.** Note the price of the device you want now, a few weeks before Black Friday, so you have something to compare with on the day. A big "save" label means little if you don't know what it normally costs.
- **Check the "was" price.** Irish consumer rules say a sale price has to be genuine: according to the [CCPC](https://www.ccpc.ie/consumer-advice/consumer-rights/buying-goods/shopping-in-the-sales), the discount must be based on the lowest price the shop charged in the previous 30 days. If a "was" price looks high, it's fair to ask the shop about it.
- **Compare across retailers.** Look at the same model in two or three Irish retailers, or on the maker's own site, before you decide.
- **Check the model and year.** An older model on offer can look like a bargain next to the newer one. Make sure it's the version you want, and that your apps run on it.
- **Look at who's selling it.** On a marketplace, check the "sold by" and "dispatched from" lines. A third-party seller has its own returns policy and delivery times, and any return goes through them.
- **Don't buy just because a timer is counting down.** If it's the right device at a fair price, buy it. If not, it'll be on offer again.

## Returns over Christmas: your rights in Ireland

These points come from the [Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (CCPC)](https://www.ccpc.ie/consumers/shopping/buying-goods/changing-your-mind/), the Irish consumer regulator:

- **Bought online:** you can cancel for any reason within 14 days of receiving the goods. The 14 days start when the parcel is delivered, not when the present is opened, so a device bought on Black Friday may be outside that window by Christmas Day.
- **Bought in a shop:** you don't have a legal right to a refund or exchange just because you changed your mind. Many shops accept returns anyway, and some extend their returns period into January, but that's their own policy. Check it before you buy and ask for a gift receipt.
- **If it's faulty:** you're entitled to a full refund if you contact the business [within 30 days](https://www.ccpc.ie/consumer-advice/consumer-rights/buying-goods/refunds), without having to accept a repair or replacement. After that you may still be entitled to a repair, replacement or refund, but the CCPC says your rights are strongest in the first 30 days.
- **Sale items:** you have the same rights as at full price, and a faulty sale item is refunded at the price you paid.
- **Gifts:** you still need proof of purchase to return a gift. For a faulty item, a gift receipt, order confirmation email or card statement can count.

## Setting it up on Christmas morning

A little preparation on Christmas Eve saves a lot of fiddling on the day:

1. **Have the Wi-Fi password ready,** written down, not just saved on someone's phone.
2. **Know which account it needs.** Fire TV uses an Amazon account, Google TV a Google account and Apple TV an Apple Account. Have the login to hand, or set it up beforehand.
3. **Check the cables and batteries.** Make sure there's an HDMI cable for a box that needs one and batteries for the remote.
4. **Plug it in and let it update.** New devices often download software the first time they're switched on. Do it on Christmas Eve if you can, so it's ready to go.
5. **Install the free Irish apps first.** RTÉ Player, Virgin Media Play and TG4 Player cover the main Irish channels. Then sign in to services the house already pays for before paying for anything new. Our [streaming prices tracker](/streaming-prices/) shows what each service costs.
6. **Check the festive sport.** Christmas is busy for football, rugby, racing and darts. Our [Sport on TV this week](/sport-on-tv/) page, updated every Thursday, shows which games are free on RTÉ, TG4 and Virgin Media and which need a paid service.
7. **If it buffers,** try moving the router or the stick, or use a wired connection. Our [buffering fixes guide]({% post_url 2026-09-11-why-is-my-streaming-app-buffering-common-fixes %}) goes through the common causes.
