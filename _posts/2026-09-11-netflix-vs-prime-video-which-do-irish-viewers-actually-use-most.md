## A Genuinely Close Race

For years, the assumption was that Netflix simply won Ireland by default. That's no longer such a safe bet. Various market-research firms that track streaming subscriptions and viewing minutes across Irish and UK households have reported Netflix and Amazon Prime Video sitting within a few percentage points of each other for overall reach or subscriber penetration, rather than Netflix holding a commanding lead.

It's worth being upfront here: I don't have a single, verified, dated figure I'm fully confident quoting down to the decimal point, and these numbers move around depending on whether a survey measures "subscribed households," "active viewers," or "minutes streamed." If you're citing a specific percentage in this post before publishing, it's worth pulling the exact figure and date from a named source (for example, a Comreg market report, a Kantar or Barb-style panel survey, or a Digital News Report Ireland/Reuters Institute study) and linking or referencing it directly, since these shifts happen quarter to quarter.

The broader point stands regardless of the exact split: this is not a Netflix landslide anymore. Prime Video's inclusion inside an Amazon Prime membership — which Irish shoppers often already pay for because of free delivery — has quietly made it a default second (or first) screen in a lot of homes.

## Why Prime Video Punches Above Its Weight

Prime Video benefits from a bundling effect that Netflix simply doesn't have. If you're already paying for Prime for parcel delivery, the video service arrives as a bonus rather than a separate line item on your bank statement. That changes the psychology of "which service do we use tonight" — people default to what feels free.

Netflix, by contrast, has to justify its subscription cost entirely on its own merits: originals, library depth, and ease of use. It still does that well for a lot of households, but it no longer has the built-in advantage of being the only credible option.

## What Irish Viewers Are Actually Comparing

When Irish viewers weigh the two, the decision tends to come down to a few practical things rather than abstract market share:

- **Original content taste**: Netflix leans heavily into a large volume of originals across genres; Prime Video has fewer originals but some high-profile ones. Personal taste matters more than raw numbers here.
- **Live sport and add-ons**: Prime Video has occasionally carried live sports rights in some markets, and both platforms increasingly push optional add-on channels or premium tiers. What's available in Ireland specifically can change, so check current listings before assuming a particular sport or channel is included.
- **Ads vs. ad-free**: Both services now offer tiers with advertising as the default or cheaper option, with ad-free available at a higher price. I'd avoid stating exact current Irish pricing here without checking the providers' own sites, since these have shifted more than once in the past couple of years.
- **Number of simultaneous streams / profiles**: This is another detail that varies by plan and has changed over time for both services, so treat any number you see quoted online as something to verify rather than take as fixed.

## Does the Device Matter?

For most Irish viewers, both apps are available across the usual smart TV platforms, streaming sticks, games consoles, and mobile devices, so device compatibility is rarely the deciding factor anymore. The bigger practical question is whether your household already owns a compatible smart TV or streaming device, and whether the interface (menu layout, search, profile switching) feels comfortable day to day. That's a subjective, hands-on thing rather than something a market-share statistic can settle for you.

## So Which Should You Choose?

If you're deciding between the two rather than paying for both, the honestly useful approach is:

1. Check which one already has content your household actually wants to watch this month, rather than relying on brand reputation.
2. Factor in whether you already pay for Prime for delivery — if so, Prime Video's marginal cost is effectively zero.
3. Look at current ad-tier and pricing pages directly on each provider's Irish site before deciding, since both have adjusted plans recently and any number printed here could be out of date by the time you read it.

## The Bigest Takeaway

The real story isn't that one service has "won" the Irish market — it's that the gap has narrowed enough that most households can reasonably justify either, or both. Anyone quoting a precise market-share percentage for Ireland should name their source and the date of the data, because this is exactly the kind of figure that looks authoritative but ages quickly.
