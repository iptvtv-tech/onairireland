Yes, a Roku is a sensible buy in Ireland if you want simple menus and the main Irish apps. RTÉ Player, Virgin Media Play, NOW and Netflix all run on Roku here; TG4 Player is the one gap we couldn't confirm.

## Roku in Ireland at a glance

- **RTÉ Player:** yes. [Roku says](https://www.roku.com/blog/rte-player-now-available-on-the-roku-platform-in-ireland) it's officially available on Roku devices in Ireland.
- **Virgin Media Play:** yes, [listed in the Roku Channel Store](https://channelstore.roku.com/details/bf33320850b76363ffa4f36a0df887ef/virgin-media-play).
- **NOW:** yes. [NOW's Irish help centre](https://www.nowtv.com/ie/help/article/what-set-top-boxes-streaming-devices-can-i-use-to-watch) supports all Roku boxes and sticks except the old Roku XS and Roku LT.
- **Netflix:** yes, on [selected Roku models](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/23886). Netflix lists which ones play in 4K and HDR.
- **TG4 Player:** not confirmed. [TG4's help page](https://www.tg4.ie/en/information/faq/) doesn't mention Roku.

## Which Roku to buy

Roku's current sticks are the Roku Streaming Stick and the Streaming Stick Plus, the 4K model. The {% include product-link.html id="roku-streaming-stick-4k" text="Roku Streaming Stick Plus (4K)" %} is listed on Amazon.ie, so you don't need to import one. Buy from an Irish or EU seller so returns and warranty are straightforward.

If you have a 4K TV and want Netflix in 4K, check the model against [Netflix's Roku page](https://help.netflix.com/en/node/23886) first. It lists model numbers rather than product names, and the newest sticks weren't on it when we checked.

## Setting up a Roku in Ireland

1. Plug the stick into a spare HDMI port and connect the power lead.
2. Connect to your Wi-Fi and create or sign in to a free Roku account with your Irish details.
3. Go to **Streaming Channels**, search for RTÉ Player, Virgin Media Play and NOW, and add them.
4. Sign in to each app with your own account.

Use Ireland as your country. Irish apps check where you are, and the right setting keeps the correct apps in the store.

## Roku vs Fire TV and Google TV

- **Roku:** the simplest menus, and the home screen doesn't push one company's shows. Suits homes that just want to open an app and watch.
- **Fire TV:** suits Alexa homes. The {% include product-link.html id="fire-tv-stick-4k-max-review" text="Fire TV Stick 4K Max" %} (2nd gen) runs Fire OS 8, but Amazon's [newer Fire TV Stick 4K (2026), Fire TV Stick HD and 4K Select run Vega OS](https://developer.amazon.com/docs/device-specs/device-specifications-comparison-table.html), which has fewer apps. We couldn't confirm that RTÉ Player, Virgin Media Play or TG4 Player are on Vega OS, so check which system a Fire TV model runs before you buy.
- **Google TV Streamer:** a small box rather than a stick, with a wired Ethernet port. See our [Google TV Streamer review](/reviews/google-tv-streamer-review-should-you-switch-from-fire-tv/).

For a wider comparison, see the [best streaming devices for Ireland](/devices/best-streaming-devices-ireland/).

## Is Roku worth it?

For most Irish homes, yes: RTÉ Player, Virgin Media Play and NOW are all there, and the menus are easy for anyone in the house. If TG4 Player is essential, search the Roku Channel Store first; you can do that on the web without owning a Roku. If you want Alexa control, a Fire OS model of Fire TV may suit you better.
