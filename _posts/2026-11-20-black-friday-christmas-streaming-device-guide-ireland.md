**The short answer:** decide which apps your house actually uses, check your TV has a free HDMI port and whether it's 4K, then pick the device that runs those apps. Look at the regular price before Black Friday so you can tell whether a "deal" is real, and keep the receipt (or ask for a gift receipt) in case it needs to go back.

We don't list prices on this page because they change from day to day over the sales. Check the current price on the retailer's own page before you buy.

Check the listing says it runs Fire OS, because Amazon's newest HD stick runs Vega OS, which has fewer apps. ## The Irish apps on each device

App lists change, so check each app before you buy rather than relying on any guide, this one included. Here's what the app stores and broadcasters themselves say:

- **RTÉ Player:** listed under Apple TV in the Irish App Store, available for Android TV on Google Play (the store Google TV devices use) and listed in Amazon's Appstore for Fire TV.
- **Fire TV models on Vega OS:** Amazon says it's adding apps to Vega, but there are fewer than on Fire OS. Search Amazon's Appstore for each app you use and check it lists your exact model.
- **Roku:** search Roku's Channel Store for RTÉ Player, Virgin Media Play and TG4 Player before you buy.
- **Virgin Media Play:** Virgin Media's own help page lists computers, Android and iOS phones and tablets, Virgin Media and Sky platforms and NOW. For a stick or box, check that device's app store.

Check any paid or sport service you use the same way. Install apps only from the device's own app store. For the full list of free Irish apps, see [5 free Irish streaming apps worth knowing]({% post_url 2026-09-23-5-free-irish-streaming-apps-worth-knowing %}).

## What to check before you buy

1. **A free HDMI port.** Every stick and box plugs into one. If the ports are taken by a set-top box or games console, you'll need to swap a cable or use a switch.
2. **Whether the TV is 4K.** Apple, for example, says you need a 4K HDR TV to get 4K HDR. On an HD TV, a 4K device just shows HD, so don't pay extra for nothing.
3. **An HDMI cable, if the box needs one.** Google and Apple both say their boxes need an HDMI cable that's sold separately. Sticks plug straight in.
4. **Wi-Fi where the TV is.** If the router is at the other end of the house, live sport is where you'll notice buffering. The Google TV Streamer and Apple TV 4K have an Ethernet port; most sticks need an adapter for a wired connection.
5. **Vega OS on new Fire TV models.** As above, check your apps are available for the exact model.
6. **Room behind the TV.** If a stick sits tight against the wall, a short HDMI extender gives it clearance.

## How to tell a real deal from a fake one

- **Know the regular price.** Note the price of the device you want now, a few weeks before Black Friday, so you have something to compare with on the day. A big "save" label means little if you don't know what it normally costs.
- **Check the "was" price.** Irish consumer rules say a sale price has to be genuine: according to the [CCPC](https://www.ccpc.ie/consumer-advice/consumer-rights/buying-goods/shopping-in-the-sales), the discount must be based on the lowest price the shop charged in the previous 30 days. If a "was" price looks high, it's fair to ask the shop about it.
- **Compare across retailers.** Look at the same model in two or three Irish retailers, or on the maker's own site, before you decide.
- **Check the model and year.** An older model on offer can look like a bargain next to the newer one. Make sure it's the version you want, and that your apps run on it.
- **Look at who's selling it.** On a marketplace, check the "sold by" and "dispatched from" lines. A third-party seller has its own returns policy and delivery times, and any return goes through them.
- **Don't buy just because a timer is counting down.** If it's the right device at a fair price, buy it. If not, it'll be on offer again.

## Returns over Christmas: your rights in Ireland

These points come from the [Competition and Consumer Protection Commission (CCPC)](https://www.ccpc.ie/consumers/shopping/buying-goods/changing-your-mind/), the Irish consumer regulator:

- **Bought online:** you can cancel for any reason within 14 days of receiving the goods. The 14 days start when the parcel is delivered, not when the present is opened, so a device bought on Black Friday may be outside that window by Christmas Day.
- **Bought in a shop:** you don't have a legal right to a refund or exchange just because you changed your mind. Many shops accept returns anyway, and some extend their returns period into January, but that's their own policy. Check it before you buy and ask for a gift receipt.
- **If it's faulty:** you're entitled to a full refund if you contact the business [within 30 days](https://www.ccpc.ie/consumer-advice/consumer-rights/buying-goods/refunds), without having to accept a repair or replacement. After that you may still be entitled to a repair, replacement or refund, and the CCPC says you're protected for up to six years, with the strongest rights in the first year.
- **Sale items:** you have the same rights as at full price, and a faulty sale item is refunded at the price you paid.
- **Gifts:** you still need proof of purchase to return a gift. For a faulty item, a gift receipt, order confirmation email or card statement can count.

## Setting it up on Christmas morning

A little preparation on Christmas Eve saves a lot of fiddling on the day:

1. **Have the Wi-Fi password ready,** written down, not just saved on someone's phone.
2. **Know which account it needs.** Fire TV uses an Amazon account, Google TV a Google account and Apple TV an Apple Account. Have the login to hand, or set it up beforehand.
3. **Check the cables and batteries.** Make sure there's an HDMI cable for a box that needs one and batteries for the remote.
4. **Plug it in and let it update.** New devices often download software the first time they're switched on. Do it on Christmas Eve if you can, so it's ready to go.
5. **Install the free Irish apps first.** RTÉ Player, Virgin Media Play and TG4 Player cover the main Irish channels. Then sign in to services the house already pays for before paying for anything new. Our [streaming prices tracker](/streaming-prices/) shows what each service costs.
6. **If it buffers,** try moving the router or the stick, or use a wired connection. Our [buffering fixes guide]({% post_url 2026-09-11-why-is-my-streaming-app-buffering-common-fixes %}) goes through the common causes.
