## A New Slogan, Not a New Service

If you've noticed NOW popping up in Irish ad breaks or on social media lately with the tagline "Now You Know", you're not imagining things. NOW has rolled out a brand refresh going into 2026, and understandably, that has some existing customers wondering if their bill, their passes, or their app are about to change too.

The short answer: this looks like a rebrand of how NOW talks about itself, rather than a rebuild of the actual product. But it's worth breaking down what a "brand refresh" typically covers, and what it doesn't, so you know what to actually watch out for.

## What "Now You Know" Seems to Be About

Based on NOW's own marketing push, the "Now You Know" campaign is centred on reassurance and clarity — leaning into the fact that NOW has no contract, no long tie-in, and lets you pick and pay for only the passes you want (Entertainment, Sky Cinema, Sky Sports, and so on, depending on what's offered in Ireland).

The messaging appears aimed at people who are unsure about streaming clutter, or who assume every streaming or TV service requires a 12- or 18-month contract the way some traditional pay-TV packages do. The campaign's job is essentially to remind people: with NOW, you know what you're paying for, you know you can cancel anytime, and you know there's no hidden lock-in.

That's a messaging exercise. It's not, as far as we can tell, a signal that NOW is changing its underlying pass structure or pricing model.

## What We Can Confirm Hasn't Changed

A few things about how NOW works day to day appear to remain the same through this rebrand:

- **It's still contract-free.** NOW has long positioned itself as a no-contract alternative to traditional pay-TV, and nothing in the rebrand announcements suggests that's changing.
- **Passes still work the same way.** You still choose the pass (or passes) relevant to what you want to watch, and stack them if you want access to multiple types of content.
- **It's still app and browser based.** NOW is accessed through its app on smart TVs, streaming sticks, consoles, phones and tablets, or via a browser — there's no indication a rebrand changes the delivery method.

## What We're Not Certain About Yet

Here's where we want to be upfront rather than guess: we don't have confirmed, current details on whether NOW's actual pass pricing in Ireland is changing alongside this rebrand, or whether menu names or app branding elements are being updated to match the "Now You Know" look.

Rebrands sometimes coincide with small pricing adjustments, introductory offer changes, or renamed tiers — but sometimes they're purely cosmetic (new logo, new ad campaign, tweaked colour palette) with zero impact on cost. Until NOW Ireland publishes or confirms specific pricing for 2026, readers should treat any numbers circulating online — including anything in older articles — as potentially out of date.

If pricing accuracy matters to you (for example, you're deciding whether to subscribe or switch passes), the safest move is to check current prices directly in the NOW app or on NOW's official website before committing, rather than relying on a screenshot or an old promotional email.

## Should Existing Customers Do Anything?

For most current NOW customers in Ireland, this rebrand likely requires no action at all. Your existing pass, your billing cycle, and your login should continue working exactly as before. Contract-free services like NOW don't typically ask customers to "re-sign" or accept new terms just because the marketing has a new tagline.

That said, it's always sensible practice to:

- Check your account settings occasionally to confirm which passes you're actively paying for.
- Cancel any pass you're not using — remember, contract-free cuts both ways, and NOW makes it straightforward to pause or stop a pass when you don't need it.
- Keep an eye on official NOW communications (email, in-app notices) rather than third-party summaries, for anything that actually affects your billing.

## The Bottom Line

"Now You Know" appears to be NOW reinforcing its core pitch — flexibility, no contracts, pay only for what you want — with a fresh look and slogan heading into 2026. Nothing we've seen so far indicates a change to how the service functions, how passes are structured, or how you interact with it day to day. Pricing specifics remain the one area worth double-checking directly with NOW before you act on them, simply because rebrands and pricing updates don't always land at exactly the same moment.
